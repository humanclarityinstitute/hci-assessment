# HCI Premium Report V2 Specification

**Status:** Locked product specification  
**Product:** HCI AI Identity & Behaviour Report  
**Report version:** V2  
**Primary principle:** **Reveal more. Explain only what improves clarity.**

---

## 1. Product purpose

The HCI premium report is a behavioural benchmarking product, not a personality profile, diagnosis, clinical instrument, or generic AI-written interpretation.

Its primary value is to show the participant:

1. where their self-reported AI behaviour sits within the HCI participant benchmark;
2. what remains distinctive after comparison with people who use AI at a similar frequency;
3. which combinations of responses define the overall profile;
4. which individual responses produced the result;
5. where their direct self-perception aligns with or differs from their assessment-based benchmark position;
6. what has been established as a dated baseline for later comparison.

The report should leave the participant able to say:

> I understand where my AI behaviour sits, what is genuinely distinctive about my pattern, which responses created it, what I had not recognised before, and what will be meaningful to compare next time.

---

## 2. Product design rules

### 2.1 Core value hierarchy

The report must prioritise:

1. measurement;
2. benchmark position;
3. comparison with similar-frequency users;
4. pattern matching;
5. question-level evidence;
6. concise synthesis;
7. longitudinal value.

Interpretation supports the measurement. It does not replace it.

### 2.2 Writing rule

> **State the measured finding strongly. Qualify the interpretation, not the evidence.**

Examples:

**Appropriate**

> Your Decision Delegation result sits at the 95th percentile within the HCI participant benchmark.

> This combination may indicate that AI input occupies a comparatively influential position within some decisions.

**Not appropriate**

> AI has reduced your ability to make independent decisions.

### 2.3 Report length

- Main report target: approximately **14–16 pages**
- Appendix: variable length, containing the complete 39-question evidence and methodology
- Main report reading time target: approximately **10 minutes**

### 2.4 Narrative economy

Every main-report section must pass at least one of these tests:

- **Discovery:** Does it reveal something the participant could not know from remembering their answers?
- **Benchmark:** Does it use HCI data a generic AI system would not possess?
- **Synthesis:** Does it make several measured findings easier to understand together?
- **Longitudinal:** Does it establish something meaningful to compare later?
- **Sharing:** Does it create a result the participant can explain or share?

Content that passes none of these tests should be shortened, moved to the appendix, or removed.

---

## 3. Benchmark terminology

Use consistently:

- **HCI participant benchmark**
- **10,000+ participant responses across 21 HCI studies**
- **assessment-based benchmark position**
- **self-reported responses**
- **participants who report using AI about as frequently as you**

Do not use report-facing language that implies a representative general population unless this is specifically established.

Avoid:

- population norm;
- population centre;
- everyone;
- objective behaviour;
- measured reality;
- observed behaviour;
- higher than X out of 100 people.

Preferred percentile wording:

> 82nd percentile within the HCI participant benchmark.

## 4. Final report structure

# Main report

## Section 1 — Cover and Important Assessment Information

**Target length:** 1 page

### Purpose

Establish the report as a dated behavioural benchmark and current reference point.

### Required content

- HCI branding
- AI Identity & Behaviour Report
- participant age band
- completion date
- report version
- benchmark version or reference date
- reported AI-use frequency
- label: **Your HCI AI Behaviour Baseline**
- full agreed personal-insight disclaimer

### Disclaimer treatment

The full disclaimer appears once on the cover in restrained publication-style typography. It must not dominate the page or appear as a large warning panel.

Full wording:

> This assessment is for personal insight only. It is not a psychological, medical or mental health assessment, diagnosis or advice, and it is not a clinical instrument. Do not rely on it for any decision that needs professional advice. If you have concerns about your wellbeing, please talk to a qualified professional.

### Intended participant reaction

> This is a credible, professionally produced measurement of where I currently sit.

---

## Section 2 — Your Signature

**Target length:** 1 page

### Purpose

Deliver the clearest, most memorable answer immediately.

### Required content

- one-sentence current-pattern signature;
- nine-dimension profile-shape visual;
- three defining signals;
- strongest valid combination;
- rarity badge where supported;
- baseline date;
- shareable composition.

### Signature rule

The signature describes the participant’s current response pattern. It must not create a permanent identity label or personality type.

Example:

> Your current responses combine extensive intellectual use of AI with strong concern about its accuracy and an unusually influential role for AI in decisions.

### Rarity rule

Rarity language may only be displayed when supported by an approved benchmark calculation or approved research estimate.

Preferred form:

> This combination appears in roughly 1 in 20 participants in the relevant HCI benchmark data.

### Intended participant reaction

> That captures my relationship with AI, and I did not realise the pattern was so unusual.

---

## Section 3 — Your Position

**Target length:** 2 pages

### Purpose

Show precisely where the participant sits across all nine assessment dimensions.

### Section framing line

> All results reflect your self-reported responses compared with the HCI participant benchmark, informed by more than 10,000 participant responses across 21 HCI studies.

### Each dimension card must contain

- dimension name;
- concise definition;
- percentile within the HCI participant benchmark;
- percentile among similar-frequency AI users;
- age-group percentile where sample size is sufficient;
- sample-size availability state;
- position visual;
- one concise comparison sentence.

### Card-writing rule

Cards explain the position. They do not contain miniature psychological essays or repeated defensive qualifications.

Example:

> Your Trust is elevated within the overall HCI participant benchmark, but closer to the benchmark centre among everyday AI users.

### Organisation

- **Defining signals**
- **Supporting signals**

### Intended participant reaction

> I know exactly where I stand and whether each result is genuinely unusual or common for someone who uses AI as often as I do.

---

## Section 4 — Compared With Similar AI Users

**Target length:** 1 page

### Purpose

Separate what is associated with usage frequency from what remains distinctive among comparable users.

### Required content

Show the three to five largest meaningful differences between:

- overall HCI participant benchmark percentile;
- similar-frequency percentile.

### Each comparison row or card contains

- dimension;
- overall percentile;
- similar-frequency percentile;
- absolute shift;
- concise meaning statement.

Example:

> High overall, but broadly typical among everyday AI users.

### Deterministic selection rule

Select comparison shifts from valid available cohort data only. Missing or underpowered cohort results must display as unavailable and must not be estimated.

### Intended participant reaction

> I can separate what is unusual about me from what is normal for someone with my level of AI use.

---

## Section 5 — What Makes You Different

**Target length:** 1–2 pages

### Purpose

Show the most informative interaction between dimensions.

### Required content

- strongest valid combination;
- both dimension positions;
- prevalence or rarity where supported;
- visual representation;
- brief explanation of the more common pattern;
- explanation of how this participant differs;
- two or three supporting question-level responses;
- concise two-paragraph interpretation.

### Interpretation structure

1. **What the data shows**
2. **What the combination may indicate**

### No-valid-combination fallback

Rename section:

> **What Makes Your Pattern Coherent**

Use a cluster of dimensions moving together rather than forcing rarity.

### Intended participant reaction

> I knew these behaviours existed separately, but I had never seen the pattern they create together.

---

## Section 6 — The Evidence

**Target length:** 2–3 pages

### Purpose

Show the strongest question-level evidence supporting the signature and profile interpretation.

### Required content

Display only the five to seven most distinctive and informative responses.

Each card contains:

- full question text;
- participant response;
- benchmark distribution;
- overall HCI benchmark percentile;
- similar-frequency percentile where available;
- related dimension;
- one concise evidence statement.

Example:

> This is one of the strongest responses contributing to your elevated Decision Delegation result.

### Exclusions

Do not include:

- paragraph-long annotations;
- new psychological interpretations;
- repeated dimension definitions;
- repeated legal caveats.

The complete 39-question profile belongs in Appendix A.

### Intended participant reaction

> I can see precisely why the report reached these conclusions. This is not generic personality writing.

---

## Section 7 — What Your Pattern Suggests

**Target length:** 1–2 pages

### Purpose

Bring the measurement together into one concise, recognisable account.

### Part A — The organising feature

A short explanation of the central tension, balance, or coherent pattern.

### Part B — How You See Yourself

Visual comparison of:

- direct self-estimate;
- assessment-based HCI benchmark position;
- direction and size of difference.

Areas:

- AI use;
- Reliance;
- Dependence-related responses.

Use **assessment-based**, not **measured versus perceived**.

### Part C — Your pattern in plain English

Target: **250–350 words**

The narrative should explain:

- the overall character of the current AI relationship;
- the strongest tension or balance;
- how the defining findings fit together;
- one clear memorable conclusion.

It must not introduce:

- diagnosis;
- unsupported causation;
- hidden psychological mechanisms;
- future prediction;
- fixed-trait language.

### Part D — Human Capital Lens

A compact three-card panel.

Each card contains:

- capability title;
- one evidence-led sentence describing why the capability is relevant within the current reported pattern.

Possible capability titles:

- Decision authorship
- Critical scepticism
- Intellectual openness
- Independent view formation
- Privacy boundaries
- Emotional discernment

The section must not claim a capability has objectively developed, declined, strengthened, or weakened.

### Intended participant reaction

> This explains the pattern clearly. It feels specific, recognisable, and grounded in my actual results.

---

## Section 8 — Dimension Reference

**Target length:** 2 pages

### Purpose

Provide a concise reference for all nine assessment dimensions.

### Each reference card contains

- dimension name;
- concise definition;
- participant percentile;
- similar-frequency position where useful;
- one participant-specific behavioural note.

### Length

Approximately **40–60 words per dimension**.

### Exclusions

Remove the previous long-form nine-dimension deep-dive essays.

### Intended participant reaction

> I understand what each dimension means and can return to this section when reviewing my profile.

---

## Section 9 — Your Baseline

**Target length:** 2 pages

### Purpose

Turn the first assessment into the beginning of a longitudinal measurement relationship.

### Page 1 — Baseline readings

Record:

- assessment date;
- report version;
- benchmark version;
- reported AI-use frequency;
- all nine dimension percentiles;
- three defining signals;
- strongest combination;
- largest self-perception difference;
- distinctive evidence references.

Heading:

> **Your HCI AI Behaviour Baseline · [date]**

### Page 2 — Your next measurement

Include:

#### Three signals to compare

Select three dimensions, relationships, or perception gaps most likely to create an informative future comparison.

These are comparison priorities, not warnings or predictions.

#### One personalised return question

A question that cannot be answered by the first assessment alone.

Example:

> Will your reported sense of decision authorship remain low relative to the amount of authority you give AI, or will these two dimensions move closer together?

#### Recommended reassessment window

> Reassess in approximately 6–12 months.

#### Future report value

State that a later assessment can show:

- what remained stable;
- what moved;
- what became newly distinctive;
- what no longer defines the profile;
- whether self-perception became more or less aligned.

### Double-delta promise

Do not promise that HCI can distinguish personal change from benchmark movement until historical benchmark versioning and reassessment linking are fully operational.

### Intended participant reaction

> I now have a credible starting point, and I genuinely want to see what changes.

---

## Section 10 — Closing

**Target length:** 1 page

### Purpose

Bookend the report with one memorable result and one unanswered question.

### Required content

- signature sentence repeated;
- personalised future measurement question;
- baseline date;
- recommended reassessment window;
- optional share or reassessment link;
- short footer disclaimer.

Footer wording:

> This assessment is for personal insight only.

### Design rule

The Closing page should visually mirror the Signature page.

### Intended participant reaction

> This is where I sit today. I want to know whether it will still be true next time.

---

# Appendix

## Appendix A — Complete 39-Question Profile

### Purpose

Preserve depth, transparency, auditability, and future-comparison detail without interrupting the main report.

### Every question contains

- full question text;
- participant response;
- HCI benchmark distribution;
- overall percentile;
- similar-frequency percentile where available;
- age-group percentile where available and sufficiently supported;
- associated dimension;
- sample availability state.

### Commentary rule

No long narrative commentary is required.

---

## Appendix B — Benchmark and Methodology

### Required content

- what the assessment measures;
- the nine dimensions;
- what percentiles mean;
- description of the HCI participant benchmark;
- participant-response count;
- cohort-comparison method;
- minimum cohort sample requirements;
- benchmark version/date;
- self-report limitations;
- interpretation boundaries;
- complete personal-insight disclaimer.

Technical qualifications should live here rather than being repeated throughout the participant-facing report.

---

## 5. Shareable result specification

A separate optional share card should be generated from the Signature page.

### May include

- profile shape;
- three defining signals;
- strongest valid combination;
- one approved benchmark comparison;
- assessment link.

### Must not include by default

- detailed emotional responses;
- Disclosure answers;
- dependence-related answers;
- sensitive question text.

The participant controls whether to share.

The card should create the natural reaction:

> I wonder where I sit.

---

## 6. Deterministic versus generated ownership

## Deterministic content

The following must be selected and rendered from calculated report data:

- dimension positions;
- cohort positions;
- defining signals;
- similar-user comparison shifts;
- rarity values;
- evidence questions;
- perception differences;
- baseline readings;
- methodology;
- all appendix content.

Claude must not invent, select, calculate, or alter these values.

## Generated content

Claude may produce only:

1. signature sentence;
2. strongest-combination narrative;
3. concise pattern narrative;
4. three Human Capital Lens items;
5. one personalised return question;
6. short baseline closing sentence.

## Target model calls

Target: **two structured Claude calls**.

### Call 1 — Profile synthesis

Returns:

- signature sentence;
- combination narrative;
- pattern narrative;
- Human Capital Lens.

### Call 2 — Baseline

Returns:

- personalised return question;
- baseline closing sentence.

Evidence captions should be deterministic unless testing proves generated captions add clear value.


## 8. Longitudinal data requirements

Store or preserve:

- report version;
- benchmark version;
- benchmark generated date;
- benchmark hash or immutable reference;
- raw responses;
- dimension scores;
- original percentile positions;
- reassessment linkage identifier;
- prior session identifier where applicable.

A future Change Report should eventually distinguish:

1. participant-response change;
2. relative-position change;
3. benchmark movement.

Do not make the full double-delta promise until this infrastructure is confirmed.

---

## 9. Legal and evidence boundaries

The report may confidently state:

- participant response values;
- benchmark percentiles;
- valid cohort comparisons;
- supported rarity;
- profile combinations;
- consistency or tension between measured response areas.

Interpretive language should use forms such as:

- may indicate;
- suggests;
- appears to reflect;
- provides one way to understand;
- is consistent with.

Avoid unsupported claims of:

- causation;
- diagnosis;
- clinical state;
- dependency as a diagnosed condition;
- objective capability loss or development;
- inevitable future movement;
- fixed identity;
- hidden psychological mechanism;
- objective truth about the participant.

Legal controls should remain largely invisible:

- full disclaimer once on cover;
- short footer line;
- technical limitations in methodology;
- safe source selection and prompt rules behind the scenes.

Use explicit report-page structures for:

- Cover;
- Signature;
- Your Position;
- Similar AI Users;
- Distinctive Pattern;
- Evidence;
- Pattern Suggests;
- Dimension Reference;
- Baseline;
- Closing.

### Print behaviour

- keep individual cards together;
- allow long multi-card sections to flow;
- do not apply `break-inside: avoid` to whole multi-page sections;
- explicitly break before Signature, Baseline, Closing, and Appendix;
- prevent isolated headings;
- preserve readable margins;
- maintain the same HTML as the online report.

### Shareable pages

Signature and Closing should work as standalone photographed pages.

## 11. Acceptance tests

The V2 report is not ready until it passes all of the following.

### Data tests

- exactly nine dimension results;
- exactly 39 appendix questions;
- five to seven main evidence questions;
- three defining signals;
- three to five similar-frequency comparison shifts where data permits;
- missing cohort data remains unavailable;
- no invented rarity;
- benchmark version stored;
- baseline date stored.

### Content tests

- no old long Human Capital section;
- no old trajectory sections;
- no nine long Deep Dives;
- no repeated legal disclaimers;
- no “population centre” or “everyone” benchmark framing;
- no “measured reality” or objective-observation framing;
- no unsupported causal or predictive claims;
- no diagnosis or clinical claims.

### Generation tests

- target two Claude calls;
- deterministic values remain unchanged by Claude;
- failure of a Claude call produces a clean deterministic fallback;
- no contradictory narrative between sections.

### Visual tests

- main report approximately 14–16 pages;
- appendix begins on a new page;
- Signature page works independently;
- Your Position pages work independently;
- What Makes You Different works independently;
- Baseline works independently;
- no unexplained blank pages or large pagination gaps;
- disclaimer is present but visually restrained.

### Product test

Print these four outputs alone:

1. Your Signature
2. Your Position / Similar AI Users
3. What Makes You Different
4. Your Baseline

Together they must communicate:

> I know where I stand.  
> I know what makes my pattern distinctive.  
> I can see the evidence behind it.  
> I have a real reason to return.

## 13. Change-control rule

No new main-report section, generated narrative block, research claim, or visual component may be added unless it clearly supports one of the product’s five value jobs:

- discovery;
- benchmark position;
- pattern synthesis;
- longitudinal value;
- sharing.

Unexpectedly large changes to scoring, benchmark calculations, research libraries, or transport infrastructure must stop for review before implementation.

The report should remain unmistakably HCI:

> **Measurement first. Pattern recognition second. Interpretation only where it adds real clarity. Longitudinal value built in from the beginning.**
